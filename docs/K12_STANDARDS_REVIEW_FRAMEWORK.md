# K–12 standards review framework

**Prepared:** 2026-07-15  
**Purpose:** A neutral worksheet for reviewing CodeKids curriculum coverage and
turning standards into age-appropriate activities.

## Research boundary

The sources below were independently selected as useful starting points, not as
the thesis author's final bibliography. The author owns the final source choice,
interpretation, argument, and any localization to the applicable national or
regional curriculum. Before publication, record the edition/date, inspect the
primary text, and replace or supplement these sources where local requirements
differ.

| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |

The K–12 Framework also publishes
[downloadable grade-band, progression, and metadata views](https://k12cs.org/downloads/),
which are preferable to manually transcribing expectations.

## Review record

Create one record per standard expectation or curriculum outcome:

| Field                | Required entry                                                                                                                 |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Source identity      | Organization, title, edition/year, URL, access date, and jurisdiction                                                          |
| Expectation          | Exact identifier plus a short paraphrase; retain a page/section locator                                                        |
| Concept/topic        | Computing systems; networks/internet; data/analysis; algorithms/programming; impacts of computing; or source-specific category |
| Practice             | The demonstrated practice, such as collaboration, abstraction, artifact creation, testing, communication, or inclusion         |
| Grade band           | Source grade band and proposed CodeKids age/grade band; never treat them as automatically equivalent                           |
| Prerequisites        | Knowledge, vocabulary, motor/reading demands, and prior activities                                                             |
| Evidence of learning | Observable behavior or artifact that would demonstrate the expectation                                                         |
| Activity mapping     | Existing activity ID, adaptation candidate, new activity, or out of scope                                                      |
| Accessibility/equity | Representation, language, device/connectivity, assistive-technology, privacy, and cultural-context checks                      |
| Review status        | Unreviewed, mapped, gap, overlap, needs evidence, approved, or intentionally excluded—with rationale                           |

## Difficulty rubric

Rate every proposed activity on all dimensions; do not use age as a proxy for
difficulty.

| Dimension                 | 1 — introductory              | 2 — developing                | 3 — proficient                       | 4 — extending                                         |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------------- | ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Concept load              | One concrete idea             | Two linked ideas              | Several interacting ideas            | Abstraction or transfer across contexts               |
| Representation            | Physical/icon blocks          | Blocks with parameters        | Mixed blocks/text or structured text | Independent text, models, or multiple representations |
| Task openness             | Imitate one demonstrated step | Complete a constrained puzzle | Design within stated constraints     | Define/refine an open problem                         |
| Reasoning                 | Recognize/sequence            | Apply/decompose               | Analyze/debug/justify                | Generalize/evaluate trade-offs                        |
| Independence              | Continuous guidance           | Scaffolds and hints           | Occasional reference support         | Plan, test, and revise independently                  |
| Literacy/interface demand | Pre-reader, one action        | Short prompts, few controls   | Multi-step instructions              | Documentation and complex tool navigation             |

An activity is age-fit only when its highest demands—including reading, working
memory, fine-motor interaction, prior experience, and emotional context—fit the
target learners or are removed through scaffolding and accessible alternatives.

## Sequence review

For each grade band, build a dependency graph rather than ordering activities by
topic name alone:

1. List prerequisite concepts and practices for each expected outcome.
2. Introduce a concept in a concrete context, practise it with variation, then
   ask learners to transfer it into a creative or authentic task.
3. Revisit core ideas in later bands with greater abstraction, independence,
   scale, and attention to trade-offs.
4. Place formative evidence before dependent activities; add remediation and an
   alternate accessible path at every decision point.
5. Check vertical continuity (no unexplained jumps) and horizontal balance
   (concepts, practices, impacts, creativity, and collaboration).

Use this compact sequence table:

| Order | Outcome/standard          | Prerequisites | Activity      | Difficulty profile  | Evidence              | Next/remediation |
| ----- | ------------------------- | ------------- | ------------- | ------------------- | --------------------- | ---------------- |
| 1     | _identifier + paraphrase_ | _none/list_   | _activity ID_ | _six rubric scores_ | _observable artifact_ | _branch IDs_     |

## Converting an expectation into an activity

1. **Unpack:** underline the action, content, context, and constraint in the
   expectation; identify the associated practice.
2. **Define evidence:** write what a learner must say, make, do, test, or explain.
   Completion alone is not evidence of understanding.
3. **Choose a child-relevant context:** preserve the cognitive goal while
   avoiding unnecessary cultural, language, or domain knowledge.
4. **Design interaction:** select physical, block, mixed, or text representation;
   add worked example, fading scaffold, feedback, and retry behavior.
5. **Create variants:** provide support, core, and extension versions that change
   scaffolding or complexity without changing the claimed outcome.
6. **Check safety and access:** minimize personal data, provide keyboard and
   assistive-technology paths, alternatives to audio/color-only cues, readable
   language, and low-bandwidth/device options.
7. **Validate:** expert-review the standards alignment, pilot with target learners,
   inspect the produced evidence, revise, and record the decision trail.

## Coverage and quality gates

A curriculum review is ready for author approval only when:

- every included standard has a traceable source and at least one evidence-producing activity;
- gaps, overlaps, exclusions, and standards supported only indirectly are explicit;
- prerequisites explain the level order and rubric scores explain difficulty;
- age fit is supported by learner observation, not only publisher labels;
- activity conversions preserve both the concept and practice in the source;
- accessibility, privacy, language, device, and cultural assumptions are documented;
- the author has verified source editions, mapping judgments, and the final argument.
